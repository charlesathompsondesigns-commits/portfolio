Meet Where We Rest.

In fall 2023 I built a small heaven on earth: laser-cut houses, hand-made trees and a koi pond, set in a garden in memory of my father.

How might a memory become a place you can visit?

Part One

Small enough to hold.

The sculpture, fall 2023

Idea

A memorial and a gift.

I wanted Where We Rest to be a memorial to my father and, at the same time, a gift.

I imagined a heaven on earth: something beautiful in its detail that would remind me of peace and love.

Our sculpture final asked each of us to choose a focus. I chose transforming materials: taking very different materials and turning them into things you see in nature and in towns, like ponds, trees and houses.

Colour

Every colour is someone.

Each house is painted a colour that stands for someone in my life who has passed on.

I wanted the houses to feel like the ones in Studio Ghibli films such as Princess Mononoke and Ponyo. Their colours make me think of spring and rebirth, and I wanted that same feeling here. Where We Rest is rooted in remembrance, and I feel these colours represent the people it remembers best. I coloured every house with an airbrush.

Houses

Cut by laser, built by hand.

I wanted the houses to look as whimsical as possible, but also clean and grand.

I knew I wouldn't have time to make every house from scratch, so I found patterns on Etsy by Ivan Bilous, then laser cut and built them myself. Each house is cut from 1/8 inch masonite, and each one took about an hour to cut and another hour to build.

Trees

Wire, hot glue and sponge.

Every tree starts as a twisted wire armature. I learned the technique from Geordie Crafts on YouTube.

I covered the wire in hot glue and textured it with a wood burner on its lowest setting until it looked like bark. Then I airbrushed the glue black and dry brushed it so the texture caught the light. The leaves are chopped up sponge.

Ground

Foam, light and a koi pond.

The base is purple insulation foam, carved with carving tools and an X-Acto knife.

I carved a track for the LED lights and holes for the tree trunks. The koi pond and its fish are sculpted from two-part epoxy clay, and I painted the fish with watered-down acrylics. SugarCharmShop on YouTube taught me how.

Finished

A garden to rest in.

I finished Where We Rest in December 2023, seven weeks after I started: four houses, four trees and a koi pond, small enough to sit on a table.

In spring 2024 it was shown in the For Women, by Women Art Gallery's 3rd Annual Exhibition at 618 S. Michigan Ave in Chicago, from April 26 to August 1.

Part Two

Big enough to walk into.

The 3D world, 2026, in progress

Vision

From a tabletop to a place.

In November 2026 I started rebuilding Where We Rest at life size, as a garden you can walk through.

The goal is a website that runs on Apple Vision Pro, so you can stand inside the garden instead of looking down at it. I'm aiming for magical realism weighted heavily toward realism: a place that could possibly exist, with low golden light like the studio video, bark detail that shines and flowers that look real. Not a cartoon, and not a photo of the miniature.

Houses

Every part, rebuilt.

I rebuilt each house in Blender from the same laser-cut files, one part at a time.

Every part is extruded to 1/8 inch, the thickness of the masonite, then fitted together the way the real house goes together. The Etsy account I bought the patterns with is closed and the instructions were never recovered, so I worked out the assembly from how the slots and tabs fit and from the seller's listing photos.

Trees

Studying real trees.

Each tree is based on a real species, studied for its bark and the way it grows.

The willow comes first: thick, gnarled limbs from a low base and long hanging shoots. The cherry blossom has a rounded crown and glossy, banded bark. The purple tree was always meant to be a tree that blooms lavender, and the yellow one is a real yellow-flowering tree fitted to the twisting trunk from the sculpture. All four now grow in the walkable garden, and I'm still refining them.

Walk

A garden you can walk through.

The garden runs in the browser with three.js.

You drag to look around and click the ground, or a house, to walk there. Scroll to rise above the garden. Each house greets you with a few lines of its own as you come near. One millimetre of the sculpture becomes five centimetres.

It's online now: step inside the garden (opens in a new tab).

Next

Still growing.

The 3D garden is still a work in progress.

The garden is online, and you can walk through it today. Next it needs testing on a real Vision Pro, and I'm still refining the details along the way.
