The world of the campaign: four rounds of concepts, the final heroes and the palette.

Meet The Deeper Bet.

Black Ambition asked me to rethink a dense infographic about their prize program for its next chapter. After four rounds of concepts, the same numbers now orbit an astronaut and the Earth: five years of proof on one side, what comes next on the other.

How might we make five years of numbers feel as big as the ambition behind them?

Brief

A staple piece for the next phase.

Black Ambition was opening a new chapter, The Deeper Bet, and wanted a staple piece to announce it.

Their infographic told the story so far: 157 prize winners funded, $348M+ in founder revenue, $297M+ in capital raised. It was heavy with text and numbers, with no clear place for the eye to land.

They wanted something aspirational and expansive, with "universe" energy, that could work on social, in presentations and on the web. They asked for two or three directions, and I had four days.

Constraint

By the book.

I worked strictly from their brand book and turned its rules into a checklist.

The rules.

Helvetica Neue and Recoleta. A ten color palette with names like Halcyon Purple and Phoenix Red. A dotted line with an exact dash spec, and a set of named gradient flares. Logo or icons, never both in one piece, and one flare per composition.

The story.

I rewrote the hierarchy before I touched a layout. The last five years became proof, condensed to a few numbers. The future became the headline: The Deeper Bet and the moves behind it. Dates came out, as the brief asked.

Three sizes from day one.

Every direction had to work as a 1080 × 1080 social post, a 1920 × 1080 blog hero and a 1920 × 640 web banner. Same vocabulary, same colors, same type. Only the composition changes.

Directions

Three ways in, then three more.

The first round stayed strictly on brand: an off-white ground, faceted forms and the dotted line.

Modular Cards gave every number its own card, a system rather than a poster. Editorial Spread told the story like a magazine feature. Trajectory turned the dotted line into an arc from the past into what comes next. My recommendation was to use all three as one campaign: lead with the editorial, extend with the cards, launch with the trajectory.

The team's notes asked for more energy. Round two moved to cream, a fine grid and a single Halcyon Purple bloom, closer to the campaign assets they were already running, and showed each direction as a main banner, a photo banner, a program grid and a newsletter post.

Stellar Cartography charted the numbers as a constellation joined by the dotted line. Monument set them at billboard scale. Horizon Architecture stacked them as columns rising into the sky. The team liked the constellation, and I refined the strongest layouts in Figma.

Pivot

Not a chart. An image.

Then the brief changed. The team didn't want the data redesigned. They wanted one powerful image that could stand in for it.

They asked for a nod to the galaxy theme and deeper, more vibrant color. So round four stopped listing the numbers and started picturing them. I kept the brand's core colors and opened the palette up into magenta, indigo and cosmic black.

The Universe Looks Like You.

A founder's silhouette filled with nebula, built around a line from Pharrell.

Generational Earth.

The Earth from deep space, with a purple bloom spreading out from a single point.

We Are the Constellation.

157 stars, one for each prize winner, with the brightest cluster forming the brand mark.

"The Universe looks like you."

Pharrell, the line behind round four
Process

Built in layers.

The final images are composites. I worked from a mix of stock and generated imagery, then built each scene up in layers.

First a clean base: the astronaut, the Earth and the light. Then the nebula. Then a color grade that pulls everything into Halcyon Purple and magenta. Then the type, and for the infographic, the numbers. Watch it build below.

Before the finals, I tried the idea in portrait posters and wide banners: the Pharrell line, The Foundation, The Deeper Bet and a newsletter post. One scene, an astronaut sitting at the edge of the Earth, went all the way to a finished hero before the team chose another direction.

Solution

The same data, retold.

Once the image was right, I brought the numbers back to it.

Prior Impact, 2021 to 2025, became one constellation, with $348M+ in founder revenue at its center. Expanding Our Impact, 2026, became another, with The Deeper Bet at its center and its five moves around it. A dotted line runs between the two, straight through the astronaut and the Earth.

It's the story their original sheet told, now with one place to start and a clear way through.

Made for Instagram.

I cut the same constellations down into a four post set: a title card, Prior Impact, Expanding Our Impact and a newsletter call. Black Ambition posted the set on Instagram ↗ on June 9, 2026, to announce The Deeper Bet.

The heroes.

Two 1920 × 1080 heroes carry the campaign where no numbers are needed: an astronaut in front of the Earth and the Earth reflected in a visor, each with and without the campaign line.

Reflection

Picture it, don't list it.

The brand rules gave me a fast, confident start. The breakthrough came when I stopped redesigning the data and started picturing it. Once the picture was right, the numbers had somewhere to live.

Four rounds in four days taught me to ask early what a team needs its audience to feel, not just what it needs to say.

Impact

Still part of the brand.

The look I designed for The Deeper Bet didn't stop at one campaign.

Black Ambition now uses my designs in many ways across the brand, from dinner card designs to check designs.
