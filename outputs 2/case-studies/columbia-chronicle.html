User Needs

Users needed a mobile-first, intuitive way to stay informed about campus news without switching between platforms or searching for content. They wanted quick access to what is new, trending, and relevant, with a seamless path from headline to full story. Beyond information, students needed content that felt culturally aligned with their campus experience and helped them feel connected to the broader Columbia College Chicago community across departments and disciplines.

Beyond improving student usability, the business goals of the Chronicle app extended to strengthening Columbia College Chicago’s broader ecosystem. The platform was intended to serve as a dynamic front door for prospective students by showcasing campus culture, creative work, and real student voices in a modern, accessible way. Internally, the app aimed to foster stronger cross-department camaraderie by highlighting stories from different majors, organizations, and initiatives, creating a shared sense of identity across disciplines. Additionally, it was designed to re-engage alumni by keeping them connected to the evolving Columbia community, encouraging interaction, pride, and long-term involvement with the college.

Purpose of These Features

What began as a simple usability issue revealed something deeper: students were not disconnected from the Chronicle because they lacked interest, but because the experience lacked cohesion. They saw stories in fragments, toggled between platforms, and felt the quiet gap between discovery and depth. The app aims to close that gap. By unifying journalism, culture, and community into one mobile-first space, it transforms scattered touchpoints into a shared campus narrative. In doing so, it answers the core pain point uncovered in research: not a need for more content, but a need for clarity, connection, and a place where Columbia’s voice feels whole.

To unify Columbia College Chicago’s journalism, culture, and community into one seamless, mobile-first platform that reduces friction, deepens engagement, and strengthens connection across students, departments, alumni, and future creatives.

1. Reduce Friction Between Discovery and Depth

Create a seamless mobile-first experience that shortens the path from headline to full story, minimizing platform switching and making it effortless for users to move from quick discovery to deeper engagement.

 

2. Increase Relevance Through Contextual Grouping and Media Surfacing

Organize content by department, identity, events, and trending topics while strategically surfacing other Chronicle media such as Photo of the Day, podcasts, and multimedia features, allowing users to engage with stories in the format that best fits their behavior.

 

3. Strengthen Community Connection Across the Columbia Ecosystem

Design a centralized hub that highlights cross-department collaboration, campus culture, alumni stories, and prospective student-facing content, positioning the Chronicle as the connective thread uniting students, faculty, alumni, and future creatives.
